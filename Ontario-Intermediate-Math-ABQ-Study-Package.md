## PART 1 — TEST & STUDY GUIDE

### 1. Understanding the Trent Entry Test
**Official information**
- Trent University states that the **Intermediate Mathematics** entry test is based on the **Ontario Grades 9 and 10 curriculum**.
- Trent's public page does **not** publish a practice test, weighting table, or guaranteed question distribution.
- This package therefore treats **Grade 9 Mathematics (MTH1W, strands B–F)** and **Grade 10 Academic Mathematics (MPM2D, 2005 curriculum)** as co-equal preparation targets.

**Study recommendation**
- Do not study Grade 9 alone.
- Use the diagnostic first, then work from highest-risk algebra/graphing/trigonometry topics outward.
- Show work on paper. An adult learner usually loses more marks from rushing than from lack of conceptual ability.

### 2. Grade 9 and Grade 10 Scope
**Official information**
- Grade 9 scope in this package: **all expectations in strands B–F** of Ontario Grade 9 Mathematics (MTH1W).
- Grade 10 scope in this package: **all Grade 10 Academic Mathematics expectations** from the 2005 Ontario curriculum section for MPM2D.

**Important note about Grade 10 expectation codes**
- The 2005 PDF organizes expectations by strand and specific bullet points. In this package, labels such as **A2.3** and **B3.2** are used as practical organizational shorthand for those specific expectations.

### 3. How to Use This Package
1. Complete the **Essential Mathematical Fluency** check without notes.
2. Complete the **Diagnostic** and mark it using the mapping table.
3. Circle the lessons attached to your weakest diagnostic items.
4. Work lesson-by-lesson: **understand → example → practice → check answers**.
5. After each major strand, complete the mixed practice without topic labels.
6. Use the **Error Log** after every practice set.
7. Finish with the **Full Curriculum Practice Assessment** and the **Readiness Checklist**.

### Lesson 1 — Grade 9 Number Sets, Density, and the Meaning of Number

#### Curriculum Connection
| Grade | Strand | Code | Expectation | Mathematical Topic | Prerequisite Knowledge | Related Expectations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 | B Number | B1.1 | Research a number concept in a cultural/community context and explain why it still matters. | History and use of number concepts | Natural, whole, integer, rational numbers | B1.2, B1.3, C1.1 |
| 9 | B Number | B1.2 | Describe subsets of a number system and compare their similarities and differences. | Subsets of number systems | Number classification and number line | B1.1, B1.3, B3.1 |
| 9 | B Number | B1.3 | Use patterns and number relationships to explain density, infinity, and limit in number sets. | Density, infinity, and limit | Fractions, decimals, number line | B1.2, C3.1 |

#### Why This Matters
Teachers preparing for the ABQ need to be fluent with classification and the logic behind number sets. This is where many later algebra errors begin.

#### Prerequisite Knowledge
Whole numbers, integers, fractions, decimals, and the number line.

#### Core Concept
Move beyond naming numbers. Understand how one set sits inside another, why rational numbers are dense, and why irrational numbers are needed.

#### Key Vocabulary
natural number; integer; rational; irrational; real number; dense; infinite; limit

#### Key Rules / Formulas
No major formula; use set inclusion notation and interval reasoning.

#### Worked Example
- **Problem:** Classify 5, -3, 2/7, 0.125, and √2 into the smallest relevant number set, then explain whether each is also real.
- **Given:** A collection of natural, integer, rational, decimal, and irrational-looking numbers.
- **Find:** The smallest number set for each value.
- **Strategy:** Rewrite each number in a simpler form if needed, then classify from smallest suitable set upward.
- **Calculation / Reasoning:**
  1. 5 is a natural number, so it is also whole, integer, rational, and real.
  2. -3 is an integer, so it is also rational and real.
  3. 2/7 is a ratio of integers, so it is rational and real.
  4. 0.125 = 1/8, so it is rational and real.
  5. √2 cannot be written as a ratio of integers, so it is irrational and real.
- **Check:** Every number listed belongs to the real numbers; only √2 is irrational.
- **Final Answer:** Smallest sets: 5 natural, -3 integer, 2/7 rational, 0.125 rational, √2 irrational.
- **Underlying reasoning:** Classification depends on the most specific true description, not the largest set containing the number.

#### Test-Level Example
- **Problem:** Explain why there are infinitely many rational numbers between 0.2 and 0.3.
- **Given:** Two decimals with 0.2 < 0.3.
- **Find:** A convincing mathematical explanation.
- **Strategy:** Produce examples and then describe a repeatable method.
- **Calculation / Reasoning:**
  1. 0.25 lies between 0.2 and 0.3.
  2. So do 0.21, 0.201, 0.2001, and 0.299.
  3. More generally, once one rational number lies between two others, its midpoint with either endpoint gives another rational number still between them.
- **Check:** Each listed value is a terminating decimal, hence rational.
- **Final Answer:** There are infinitely many rational numbers between 0.2 and 0.3 because midpointing or refining decimal places always creates another rational number in the interval.
- **Underlying reasoning:** Rational numbers are dense: between any two distinct real numbers, another rational number exists.

#### Common Mistakes
Confusing “integer” with “whole number”; believing no numbers lie between close decimals; assuming repeating decimals are irrational.

#### Practice Questions
1. Classify -8, 0, 4/5, π, and √49 by the smallest suitable set.
2. State whether each statement is true: all integers are rational; all rational numbers are integers; all irrational numbers are real.
3. Write two rational numbers and one irrational number between 1 and 2.
4. Explain in one sentence why 0.333... is rational.
5. A student says there is no number between 0.45 and 0.46. Refute the claim.
6. Research prompt: pick zero, π, or negative numbers and note one cultural/historical milestone plus one modern use.

#### Core Concept
Use patterns, not memorized slogans alone. Positive, zero, and negative exponents all follow the same multiplication/division logic.

#### Key Vocabulary
base; exponent; power; reciprocal; scientific notation

#### Key Rules / Formulas
a^m·a^n=a^(m+n); a^m/a^n=a^(m-n); (a^m)^n=a^(mn); a^0=1 for a≠0; a^-n=1/a^n

#### Worked Example
- **Problem:** Simplify (2^3)(2^-5).
- **Given:** Same base, so exponent laws apply.
- **Find:** A simplified exact value.
- **Strategy:** Add exponents when multiplying like bases.
- **Calculation / Reasoning:**
  1. (2^3)(2^-5) = 2^(3 + -5) = 2^-2.
  2. 2^-2 = 1/2^2 = 1/4.
- **Check:** 2^3 = 8 and 2^5 = 32, so 8/32 = 1/4.
- **Final Answer:** 1/4.
- **Underlying reasoning:** A negative exponent means reciprocal, not a negative value.

#### Test-Level Example
- **Problem:** Write 0.000456 in scientific notation.
- **Given:** A small decimal less than 1.
- **Find:** The form a × 10^n with 1 ≤ a < 10.
- **Strategy:** Move the decimal to create a number between 1 and 10, then compensate with a power of ten.
- **Calculation / Reasoning:**
  1. Move the decimal 4 places right: 0.000456 → 4.56.
  2. Because the decimal moved right, the exponent is negative.
  3. Therefore 0.000456 = 4.56 × 10^-4.
- **Check:** 10^-4 = 0.0001, and 4.56 × 0.0001 = 0.000456.
- **Final Answer:** 4.56 × 10^-4.
- **Underlying reasoning:** Scientific notation records both size and place value efficiently.

#### Common Mistakes
Dropping the sign on a negative exponent; confusing (-3)^2 with -3^2; multiplying exponents when multiplying like bases.

#### Practice Questions

2. Simplify x^3·x^5.
3. Simplify y^7/y^2.
4. Simplify (3a^2)^2.

- **Underlying reasoning:** Coordinate geometry verifies figure properties numerically.

#### Test-Level Example
- **Problem:** Show that A(0,0), B(4,2), C(2,6), and D(-2,4) form a parallelogram.
- **Given:** Four vertices in order.
- **Find:** A coordinate property proving a parallelogram.
- **Strategy:** Compare slopes of opposite sides.
- **Calculation / Reasoning:**
  1. Slope AB=(2-0)/(4-0)=1/2.
  2. Slope CD=(4-6)/(-2-2)=-2/-4=1/2.
  3. Slope BC=(6-2)/(2-4)=4/-2=-2.
  4. Slope AD=(4-0)/(-2-0)=4/-2=-2.
- **Check:** Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
- **Final Answer:** The quadrilateral is a parallelogram.
- **Underlying reasoning:** A parallelogram can be verified by showing both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.

#### Common Mistakes
Using one property when two are needed; proving equal diagonals and assuming the shape must be a square; arithmetic slips in slope or distance.

#### Why This Matters
Similarity drives scale reasoning, indirect measurement, and the logic behind trigonometric ratios.

#### Prerequisite Knowledge
Ratios, proportions, angle facts, and triangle vocabulary.

#### Core Concept
Similar triangles have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding side lengths. Congruent triangles are a special case with scale factor 1.

#### Key Vocabulary
similar; congruent; scale factor; corresponding sides; corresponding angles

#### Key Rules / Formulas
corresponding side ratios are equal in similar triangles

#### Worked Example
- **Problem:** Triangles with side lengths 3,4,5 and 6,8,10 are similar. State the scale factor.
- **Given:** Two side-length sets.
- **Find:** Whether the ratios match and the scale factor.
- **Strategy:** Compare corresponding side ratios.
- **Calculation / Reasoning:**
  1. 6/3=2.
  2. 8/4=2.
  3. 10/5=2.
- **Check:** All three ratios match.
- **Final Answer:** The triangles are similar with scale factor 2 from the first to the second.
- **Underlying reasoning:** Similar figures preserve angle measure and proportional side lengths.

#### Test-Level Example
- **Problem:** A 2 m object casts a 1.5 m shadow; a tree casts a 6 m shadow. Find the tree height.
- **Given:** Same sun angle creates similar triangles.
- **Find:** The unknown height h.
- **Strategy:** Set up a proportion using corresponding sides.
- **Calculation / Reasoning:**
  1. h/6 = 2/1.5.
  2. h = 6(2/1.5)=8.
- **Check:** The tree shadow is 4 times the object’s shadow, so height should be 4 times 2 m.
- **Final Answer:** The tree is 8 m tall.
- **Underlying reasoning:** Similar triangles allow indirect measurement.

#### Common Mistakes
Matching sides in the wrong order; assuming equal area scale instead of linear scale; confusing congruent with similar.

#### Why This Matters
Right-triangle trig is a high-priority skill. It combines geometry, ratios, equation-solving, and interpretation.

#### Prerequisite Knowledge
Pythagorean theorem, similar triangles, and calculator fluency in degree mode.

#### Core Concept
SOH-CAH-TOA is a memory aid, but the real idea is ratio invariance in similar right triangles.

#### Key Vocabulary
sine; cosine; tangent; angle of elevation; angle of depression; adjacent; opposite; hypotenuse

#### Key Rules / Formulas
sin θ=opp/hyp, cos θ=adj/hyp, tan θ=opp/adj

#### Worked Example
- **Problem:** In a right triangle, θ=35° and the hypotenuse is 12 cm. Find the side opposite θ.
- **Given:** Angle and hypotenuse.
- **Find:** Opposite side.
- **Strategy:** Use sine because sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse.
- **Calculation / Reasoning:**
  1. sin35° = opposite/12.
  2. opposite = 12sin35° ≈ 6.88.
- **Check:** The opposite side should be shorter than the hypotenuse, which it is.
- **Final Answer:** About 6.9 cm.
- **Underlying reasoning:** Primary trig ratios compare side lengths in similar right triangles.

#### Test-Level Example
- **Problem:** A 5 m ladder makes a 68° angle with the ground. Find the height reached.
- **Given:** Hypotenuse 5 and an angle with the ground.
- **Find:** Vertical height.
- **Strategy:** The height is opposite the given angle, so use sine.
- **Calculation / Reasoning:**
  1. sin68° = height/5.
  2. height = 5sin68° ≈ 4.64.
- **Check:** The height is slightly less than the ladder length, which is reasonable.
- **Final Answer:** About 4.6 m.
- **Underlying reasoning:** Applied trig problems begin with a correct sketch and side-labeling.

#### Common Mistakes
Choosing the wrong ratio; using radian mode accidentally; confusing angle of depression with the interior triangle angle.

### Lesson 21 — Grade 10 Trigonometry of Acute Triangles: Sine Law and Cosine Law

#### Curriculum Connection
| Grade | Strand | Code | Expectation | Mathematical Topic | Prerequisite Knowledge | Related Expectations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | C Trigonometry | C3.1 | Explore why the sine law works. | Development of the sine law | Area of triangles and trig ratios | C3.2, C3.3 |

| 10 | C Trigonometry | C3.3 | Determine unknown sides and angles in acute triangles using the sine law or cosine law. | Solving acute triangles | C3.2 and calculator fluency | C3.2, C3.4 |
| 10 | C Trigonometry | C3.4 | Explore why the cosine law generalizes the Pythagorean theorem. | Development of the cosine law | Pythagorean theorem and algebra | C3.1, C3.2, C3.3 |

#### Why This Matters
Acute-triangle trigonometry tests whether you can choose a method rather than follow a labelled topic. That makes it strong entry-test preparation.

#### Prerequisite Knowledge
Lesson 20, algebraic rearrangement, and careful diagram reading.

#### Core Concept
Use the sine law when you know an angle-side opposite pair. Use the cosine law when the data involve SAS or all three sides.

#### Key Vocabulary
sine law; cosine law; included angle; ambiguous case

#### Key Rules / Formulas
a/sin A=b/sin B=c/sin C; c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab cos C

#### Worked Example
- **Problem:** In △ABC, a=8, A=40°, b=11. Find B using the sine law.
- **Given:** A known angle-side opposite pair.
- **Find:** Angle B.
- **Strategy:** Use a/sinA = b/sinB.
- **Calculation / Reasoning:**
  1. 8/sin40° = 11/sinB.
  2. sinB = 11sin40°/8 ≈ 0.884.
  3. B ≈ sin^-1(0.884) ≈ 62.1°.
- **Check:** The angle is acute and fits the side lengths given.
- **Final Answer:** B ≈ 62.1°.
- **Underlying reasoning:** The sine law connects sides to their opposite angles.

#### Test-Level Example
- **Problem:** In △ABC, a=7, b=9, C=52°. Find c using the cosine law.
- **Given:** Two sides and the included angle.
- **Find:** The third side.
- **Strategy:** Use c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab cosC.
- **Calculation / Reasoning:**
  1. c^2=7^2+9^2-2(7)(9)cos52°.
  2. c^2=49+81-126cos52° ≈ 130-77.57 ≈ 52.43.
  3. c≈√52.43 ≈ 7.24.
- **Check:** The side length is plausible between |9-7| and 9+7.
- **Final Answer:** c ≈ 7.2.
- **Underlying reasoning:** The cosine law generalizes the Pythagorean theorem to non-right triangles.

#### Common Mistakes
Using sine law without an opposite angle-side pair; ignoring the possibility of two sine-law solutions; failing to round only at the end.

## PART 12 — RAPID REVIEW SHEET

### Number and Arithmetic
- **Percent of a quantity:** percent × whole. Convert percent to a decimal first.
- **Percent change:** (change/original) × 100%.
- **Rate:** compare two different units, e.g. km/h.
- **Proportion:** two equal ratios; solve by cross multiplication when appropriate.
- **Exponent rules:** add exponents when multiplying like bases, subtract when dividing like bases, multiply exponents for a power of a power.

### Algebra
- **Equivalent expressions:** different-looking expressions can represent the same quantity.
- **Distributive property:** a(b+c)=ab+ac.
- **Linear equation:** solve by isolating the variable while preserving balance.
- **Equation of a line:** often use y=mx+b, where m is slope and b is y-intercept.
- **Slope:** rise/run = (y2-y1)/(x2-x1).

### Data and Modelling
- **Median** is resistant to outliers.
- **IQR** measures the spread of the middle 50% of the data.
- **Correlation** describes relationship strength/direction, not cause.
- **Models** are useful approximations, not perfect copies of reality.

### Geometry and Measurement
- **Pythagorean theorem:** a^2+b^2=c^2 for right triangles.
- **Area scaling:** multiply by the square of the linear scale factor.
- **Volume scaling:** multiply by the cube of the linear scale factor.
- **Cylinder volume:** V=πr^2h.
- **Cone/Pyramid volume:** V=(1/3)Bh.

### Financial Literacy
- **Simple interest:** I=Prt.
- **Compound growth:** repeated multiplication by the growth factor.
- **Budgeting:** income - expenses - savings target should not be negative.

### Quadratics
- **Standard form:** y=ax^2+bx+c.
- **Vertex form:** y=a(x-h)^2+k, vertex (h,k), axis x=h.
- **Axis of symmetry from standard form:** x=-b/(2a).
- **Quadratic formula:** x=[-b±√(b^2-4ac)]/(2a).
- **Discriminant:** b^2-4ac; tells the number of real roots.

### Analytic Geometry
- **Distance formula:** √[(x2-x1)^2+(y2-y1)^2].
- **Midpoint formula:** ((x1+x2)/2,(y1+y2)/2).
- **Origin-centred circle:** x^2+y^2=r^2.
- **Parallel lines:** equal slopes.
- **Perpendicular lines:** slopes are negative reciprocals when both are defined.

### Trigonometry
- **sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse**
- **cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse**
- **tan θ = opposite/adjacent**
- Use **sine law** when you know an opposite angle-side pair.
- Use **cosine law** for SAS or SSS situations.
